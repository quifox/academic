Sharp Cyclic Forgetting in Linear RegressionPreprint

Jiangze Zhu

2026

Abstract

Sequentially fitting jointly realizable linear-regression tasks in a fixed cycle can reintroduce residual error on tasks that were fitted exactly. We characterize the largest such endpoint error under orthogonal-projection updates. For T tasks and m complete cycles, when q is the minimum dimension of a task solution subspace, the worst-case average squared residual is Θ(min{1, T·min(T,q)/m}), uniformly over finite-dimensional task families. This identifies a transition from linear to quadratic dependence on the task count as q increases. We also determine the exact leading constant in the unrestricted iterated asymptotic regime: as m and then T tend to infinity, m times the worst-case average squared residual, divided by T squared, tends to 1/(2π⁴). The corresponding constant for the maximum task residual is 1/(8π²). The proof connects the cycle product and endpoint observations through closed paths of subspaces. A sharp dissipative semigroup bound provides the upper constant, while a finite real projection construction matches it. The argument distinguishes endpoint residuals from the norm decrease in the following cycle, a distinction essential for the lower bound. The result gives a worst-case characterization of exact cyclic fitting without assumptions on how task subspaces are distributed.
